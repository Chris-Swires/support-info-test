The headline

Hidden in the average
Two extensions look fine on the blended number. One of them isn't — the other is just noisy.
This is why the worst-check verdict above matters: a slow write check can regress badly and still barely move a pooled percentile, because it simply completes fewer times in a fixed-duration run.

Australia — a real hidden regression

Blended p95 (looks fine)-1%

Australia's blended figure says “no material change”. One write check underneath it more than tripled in p95 latency. Both are true at once — that's the point of checking worst-case, not just the average.

Denmark — a low-sample-size scare, not a regression

Blended p95 (looks fine)+6%
Worst check by median: Create concept+2578%

That same check's p95 barely moved (+15%). With only 20 samples after upload, one or two slow requests can swing a median by thousands of percent without reflecting a real, repeatable slowdown. Treat large median-only moves on small samples with more scepticism than p95 moves.

So what
Recommendation
  • Treat this as a real, service-wide slowdown — not a rounding error. Programme-wide p95 is up +57% and median is up +75%; throughput is down +14%.
  • Netherlands needs attention first — every check regressed, several by more than 250%. This is the one extension where the picture is unambiguous.
  • Don't be reassured by a healthy-looking blended number alone — Australia shows why: a tripled write-latency check hides behind a flat overall p95.
  • Don't over-react to median swings on small samples either — Denmark's scariest-looking number is very likely noise from a ~20-sample check, not a real regression.
  • The UK extension itself is unremarkable — it ranks 4 of 5 on both metrics: mid-pack, not an outlier in either direction.
Caveats: single run per configuration (no repeat runs yet, so no error bars on any figure above); UKTEST-4's signed-off KPI thresholds were not yet available when this suite ran; before/after also compares two different hosting instances, not the UK extension upload alone.
